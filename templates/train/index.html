The doctrine

What the session is built on.

Four ways in. Same belief underneath.

When you choose to love it, you choose the weather.

You spend time doing the things you love. So if you are going to apply yourself to training, you have to love it — the fun, the frustration, the winning, the goals, and finding out who you actually are as a player.

Once you get older and around better players, the margin for technical difference becomes minuscule. The mental side — playing with freedom in tense situations — is what separates one player from another. That, paired with knowing this is a game, and a tool used to develop you for the rest of your life.

No getting high on the highs. No getting too low on the lows. When you choose to love it, you choose to accept both as the thing that makes you better.

Once you are the best on the team, it is getting to be time to go. Mother's Law

Be the best in the neighbourhood first. The country second. You are a player inside a large pool — know the next step, rise to the top of this one, move, repeat.

And keep training in the off hours you aren't with your team. That is where the difference is actually made.
